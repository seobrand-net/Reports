September spend rose 10% to $6,622, and paid leads in WhatConverts rose 25%, from 36 to 45, so cost per lead fell 12% to $147. 01Notes

Reading this report

What each number means and where it comes from.

Two counts

Why Google Ads shows 33 conversions and WhatConverts 45 leads

WhatConverts counts every paid call and form. Google Ads counts only the ones it can tie to an ad click, once per click, on the date of the click.

In September, call-extension calls that WhatConverts sent to Google Ads (6) were tracked as secondary conversions, so they appear under All conv. and not in the Conversions column. Since October 27 they count in Conversions too.

Lead quality

What the classification means

New prospect: a business asking about service or a quote. Existing customer: a current client calling about pickups, billing or paperwork. Non-sales / other: vendors, job seekers and similar calls. Too short to analyze: brief calls (mostly under 30 seconds) that WhatConverts did not analyze.

Call classification comes from the WhatConverts call analysis. Every form is a quote request; the three Allentown Family Dental forms on Sep 17 are one request submitted three times.

Impression share

How visible the ads were

Impression share is the percentage of eligible searches where an ad showed. The rest was lost for one of two reasons: budget (the daily budget ran out) or rank (the bid or ad quality was too low to win the auction).

Performance Max reports impression share for its Search ads only. Its channel split comes from Google's Performance Max channel report.

Call extension

Calls without a campaign

Six calls came through the Google call extension and could not be matched to a single campaign, so the Lead Report lists them as Google Call Extension.
